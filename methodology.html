§1

Mass balance: stage 1

Split the feed into water, organic solids and mineral solids:

water W₀ = 75,000 × (1 − 0.63) = 27,750 t
dry solids DS₀ = 75,000 × 0.63 = 47,250 t
organic DS O₀ = 47,250 × 0.44 = 20,790 t  mineral M = 26,460 t

WTT's losses turned into rates, so they scale when throughput changes:

evap. rate e₁ = 13,022 ÷ 27,750 = 46.93% of water
degr. rate d₁ = 2,068 ÷ 20,790 = 9.95% of ODS
Out₁ = 75,000 − 13,022 − 2,068 = 59,910 t/yr
check DS (18,722 + 26,460) ÷ 59,910 = 75.4% (WTT shows 75%) ✓
§2

Mass balance: stage 2 + heat

e₂ = 4,814 ÷ 14,728 = 32.69%   d₂ = 764 ÷ 18,722 = 4.08%

WTT's "additional heat −1,847 t/yr" is water driven off by heat:

latent heat 2,500 kJ/kg ÷ 3.6 = 694.4 kWh per t water
1,282,814 kWh ÷ 694.4 = 1,847 t ✓
per tunnel 1,282,814 ÷ (156 batches × 4 d × 24 h) = 85.7 kW
Out₂ = 59,910 − 4,814 − 764 − 1,847 = 52,485 t/yr (WTT 52,486)
reduction = 1 − 52,485 ÷ 75,000 = 30.02% ✓   DS₂ = 84.6%
Caution: WTT counts degradation as a solids-only loss. Real composting also makes water, so actual evaporation and output moisture will be a bit higher.

§3

Batch per tunnel

Every stage 1 tunnel turns once a week:

batches = 52 wk × 3 tunnels = 156 /yr
stage 1 load = 75,000 ÷ 156 = 481 t   → 481 ÷ 0.35 = 1,374 m³
stage 2 load = 59,910 ÷ 156 = 384 t   → 1,097 m³
stage 2 out = 52,485 ÷ 156 = 336 t   → 961 m³

Filling height. The first 1.5 m behind the door holds no pile. This value is back-calculated so all 4 of WTT's filling heights match:

stage 1 floor = (40 − 1.5) × 8 = 308 m²   → 1,374 ÷ 308 = 4.46 m < 4.5 ✓
stage 2 floor = (33 − 1.5) × 8 = 252 m²   → 1,097 ÷ 252 = 4.35 m < 4.5 ✓
capacity at 4.5 m 4.5 × 308 × 0.35 = 485 t  (only 4 t spare per fill)

§5

Mon–Sat turning plan

Turning always goes into a different tunnel. The stage 2 tunnel is emptied first, so it is the "2nd tunnel available":

dayorder of work
Mon① empty T4 → output 336 t, then ② turn T1 → T4, 384 t
Tue③ fill T1 from the detection bay, 481 t
Wed / Thusame for T2 → T5, refill T2
Fri / Satsame for T3 → T6, refill T3
Sunno loading · bay under camera watch
stage 1 time = Tue refill → Mon turn = 6 days   stage 2 time = Mon → Mon = 7 days
WTT's rates assume 7 + 7 days. We run 6 + 7. Ask WTT to confirm that stage 2 output still meets spec.

Build 2: the same days, with 2 tunnels each (T1+T2 → T7+T8 on Mon, and so on).

Loader (9 m³ bucket)

fill: 1,374 ÷ 9 = 152.6 → 153 buckets × 2 min = 5.1 h (Tue/Thu/Sat)
empty: 961 ÷ 9 → 107 · turn: 1,097 ÷ 9 → 122 · total 229 × 2 min = 7.6 h (Mon/Wed/Fri)
week = 3 × 153 + 3 × 229 = 1,146 buckets = 38.2 h
year = 38.2 × 52 = 1,986 h/yr > 1,600 h ✗
→ 2 loaders, split by task:
L2 empty stage 2 = 107 × 2 min × 3 × 52 = 556 h + shredder feed 240 t ÷ 0.35 ÷ 9 → 77 buckets/day = 801 h → 1,357 h
L1 fill + turn = 1,986 − 556 = 1,430 h   both ≤ 1,600 h ✓
+ DH-01 hopper feed 52,485 ÷ 0.35 ÷ 9 × 2 min = 555 h → total 3,343 h → 2 loaders at ~1,670 h (4% over) or 3
output OS-01 storage bays: peak 1 tunnel = 336 t / 961 m³ → 3 × 320 m³ · DH-01 60 m³ ≈ 38 min at 32.8 t/h
SP-01 1,600 h: 52,485 ÷ 1,600 = 32.8 t/h needed (WTT output basis)
chosen: 33 t/h × 1,600 h = 52,800 t/yr ≥ 52,485 ✓ (0.6% margin; 25 t/h would leave 12,485 t short)
Build 2 tunnels 3,973 h + feed/emptying ≈ 5,560 h ÷ 1,600 → 4 loaders

Raw MSW density (0.35) and the 2-minute cycle are assumed. Bulky feed to SH-02 is assumed to use its own grab.

Shredder: 1,600 h for MSW + bulky

bulky = 60,000 t ÷ 20 t/h = 3,000 h/yr (9.6 h/day Mon–Sat)
3,000 ÷ 1,600 = 1.9 × the whole shredder year ✗ → MSW gets 0 h
one machine doing both, MSW at 50 t/h (example):
3,000 + 75,000 ÷ 50 = 3,000 + 1,500 = 4,500 h/yr = 14.4 h/day Mon–Sat
optionwhat it means
✓ 2nd shredder for bulkySH-02 at ≥ 38 t/h fits 1,600 h (1,500 h at 40 t/h). MSW keeps SH-01: 1,600 h at 46.9 t/h
double shift~4,500 h/yr on one machine. Check maintenance time

60,000 t of bulky waste is 80% of the MSW tonnage. That's unusually high, so check it isn't the whole site intake. If it's right, the shredder is the bottleneck, not the tunnels.

Chosen: SH-01 MSW only, 12:00–17:08. SH-02 bulky only, 07:00–11:48. Each backs up the other. Bulky output is assumed not to go to the tunnels.

§8

Detection bay after the shredder

Idea: batteries damaged by the shredder get a chance to go into thermal runaway in an open, camera-watched bay, not inside a closed tunnel.

stage A: Blazequel on the SH-01 outfeed belt catches ignitions within seconds to minutes
stage B: the bay catches delayed ignitions (hours) · no picking station, so every battery reaches the shredder

12 h hold, checked hour by hour:

1 fill (481 t) = 481 ÷ 240 = 2 days of shredding
fill 07:00 → 12:06, then shred 12:00 → 17:08 into the empty bays
youngest load at fill = 17:08 → 07:00 = 13.9 h ≥ 12 h ✓
peak = 2 days only (no overlap with today's shred) = 481 t
→ 481 ÷ 0.35 = 1,374 m³ (size it at 350 kg/m³)
1 day's shred = 240 t = 687 m³ = 1 pair of bays → pair A / pair B alternate
watch time: min 13.9 h · max 66 h (Sat → Tue)
Comparison: shredding in the morning gives 528 t (4 × 377 m³). A 24 h hold gives 721 t (5 bays). Shredding in the afternoon and filling in the morning is the smallest bay possible without splitting tunnel fills.
pile height = 4.3 wall − 0.5 = 3.8 m
floor = 1,374 ÷ 3.8 = 361 m² (24 h: 542 m²)
bays = 1,374 ÷ 450 m³ pile limit = 3.05 → 4 bays of 343 m³ (~90 m² each)
Build 2 962 t · 2,747 m³ · 723 m² · 7 bays (24 h: 10 bays)

Batteries at 1 kg/t (0.1% by weight):

per day = 240 t × 1 = 240 kg   per fill = 481 × 1 = 481 kg
in bay at peak = 481 × 1 = 481 kg of batteries   per year = 75 t
count ÷ 0.05 kg each (assumed) ≈ 4,800 per day (20 per tonne)
1 kg/t is a planning figure in the 0.5–2 kg/t range for loose batteries and vapes in residual MSW. Replace it with a battery audit of your own feed. Even at this level there are thousands of units a day, so the bay is still needed.
