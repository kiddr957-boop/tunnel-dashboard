Fire strategy · post-shredding detection bay

Shredded waste waits in an open, camera-watched bay for at least the hold time before any tunnel is filled. A cell damaged by the shredder that goes into thermal runaway should then do it where it can be seen and put out, not inside a closed tunnel.

Model basis
  • The mass balance uses the WTT 3-tunnel sheet as rates: water evaporation as a % of water in, organics degradation as a % of organic DS in, and additional heat evaporating water at 2,500 kJ/kg. Build 2 doubles the throughput and tunnel count at the same tunnel sizes.
  • Every stage 1 tunnel turns once a week. Batch = annual tonnage ÷ (operating weeks × stage 1 tunnels), which is WTT's 481 t for Build 1.
  • Split-day pattern: waste spends 6 days in stage 1 (refill day to turning day) and 7 days in stage 2. WTT's rates assume 7 + 7, so real stage 1 losses may be slightly lower.
  • Density applies to both the shredded feed and the stage 1 output going into stage 2 (WTT uses 0.35 t/m³ for both). The dead length of 1.5 m is back-calculated from WTT's filling heights.
  • Shredder: MSW is shredded in the set h/yr, spread evenly Mon–Sat in the first part of the shift. The remaining available hours are for bulky waste, which is assumed not to go to the tunnels.
  • The detection bay is simulated day by day, first in, first out. Deliveries are shredded the day they arrive. Tunnels are filled late in the shift, so the previous day's shredding has had the full 24 h hold. The bay peak is the morning stock plus that day's shredding. Filling early in the morning instead would need a 2-day hold for a true 24 h and a bigger bay.
  • Pile limit and fire-water rate follow the style of the English EA Fire Prevention Plan guidance. Coleraine is regulated by NIEA, so confirm the values that apply. Inputs marked assumed are not from WTT; replace them with site data.
