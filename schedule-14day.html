Why Tue / Thu / Fri

  1. The rule: with a 14-day cycle, a tunnel's unload always falls the day before its load weekday. Loading on Monday would put an unload on Sunday.
  2. 6 tunnels ÷ 14 days = 3 loads a week of 481 t, exactly WTT's stage 1 batch, at 4.46 m in a 40 m tunnel.
  3. Of the four ways to avoid weekend work entirely, Tue/Thu/Fri (or Tue/Wed/Fri) keeps the detection bay smallest: 721 t.
  4. No turn: each batch stays in one tunnel for 14 days. That saves ~634 loader-h/yr, so tunnel work + shredder feed + hopper = ~2,708 h → 2 loaders at ~1,354 h, both under 1,600 h.

Check with WTT before adopting

  1. This is a single-pass 14-day cycle with no turn. WTT's mass balance assumes 7 + 7 days with a transfer between tunnels (remixing, rewetting, extra heat in phase 2). Without the turn, final dry solids, stability and the heat plan may change.
  2. Heat: WTT applies extra heat on days 1–4 of phase 2. In a single pass, when and how much heat is needed must be redefined.
  3. Weekend days count as dwell days, so the 12 days run straight through Saturday and Sunday. The cost of no weekend work is one extra detection bay and one extra output bay compared with loading on Saturday.
